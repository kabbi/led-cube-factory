# Hexagon tiles

2026-10-16. Result: `hex30-5050x37-p8-pvia`, `hex30-2020x169-p3.7-pvia`.

**Question.** Hexagons in 5050 and 2020, with the best LED count and pitch.

Same 30 mm edge, so a hexagon mates with every other tile (truncated
octahedron: squares + hexagons; truncated tetrahedron: triangles +
hexagons). LEDs sit on a hex lattice in `rings` (1, 7, 19, 37, ...
3n(n-1)+1), rows parallel to the top and bottom edges. All six sides of the
lattice face an edge, so towards the four slanted edges a square package
reaches 1.37 x half its size (the 2020's pads: 1.55 mm). For each ring count,
the widest pitch that keeps that clear of the connector pads:

| board | LEDs | pitch | to the pads | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `hex30-5050x37-p8-pvia` | 4 rings, 37 | 8.0 mm (max ~8.05) | 0.4 mm | 5 rings need ~6 mm: bodies 0.2 mm apart |
| `hex30-2020x169-p3.7-pvia` | 8 rings, 169 | 3.7 mm (max ~3.77) | 0.6 mm | same LED density as the 8x8 at 3.4 mm |

## The back is the hard part

Each of the six ports has a jumper on all three hubs, so port nets and hubs
form a K3,6, which needs at least six crossings: In2 hops, each needing two
via spots free on every layer. The 5050 board and the 2020 at 3.7 mm have
them; a 2020 field at 3.3-3.4 mm doesn't. Hubs: `jumper_radius: 4.3` for six
jumpers, `radius: 8` keeps the three rings apart.

Generated back, patterned vias:

| board | change | result |
|---|---|---|
| 5050, 4 rings, 8.0 mm | default rules and hubs | **DRC clean**, 62/74 vias patterned, ~135 s |
| 2020, 9 rings (217), 3.3 mm, shallow | 0.127/0.1, hole-copper 0.2 | P2 boxed in on the back |
| 2020, 9 rings, 3.3 mm, shallow | same, hubs angle 240 (IN by D1, OUT by the last LED) | P2 boxed in |
| 2020, 8 rings (169), 3.4 mm | default rules, hubs angle 0 / 240 | P2 / P3 boxed in |
| 2020, 8 rings, 3.7 mm | default rules or 0.127/0.1, hubs angle 0 or 240 | all four route (~400 s run 4 at once); default rules + hubs: **DRC clean**, 329/338 patterned, ~180 s alone |

## Side effects

- The generated hex board is 3.5x the square's area, and so is the router's
  grid: the first builds hung for 12+ minutes. `Grid._window` now prepares
  its shapes before testing grid points (~40x faster on board-sized polygons
  like the data corridors; same result).
- The short perpendicular silk lines at the ends of `edge_conn` blended into
  a square's outline but drew "arrows" at a hexagon's vertices; removed.
- Panels: hexagons in a grid meet corner to corner, so tabs go on the flat
  top and bottom edges only (3 joints per column, 18 tabs). A honeycomb
  (columns offset by half a board) saves no area at six boards. Six 60 x 52 mm
  hexes can't fit 100 x 100 mm.
